## 4 · Verdict

### Is §4 buildable cold?

**No — not as written.** §4 is the strongest-sourced section in the document (its engine claims check out against real receipts almost perfectly), and that is exactly what makes its gaps dangerous: a cold session will trust it and build straight past six undefined seams. Specifically, a session with only this repo cannot answer:

1. **What is a boundary on the plant lane?** (M-B6) — the judgment cadence, the Deadline Law's grain, and the probe budget all hang off this, and the inherited segmenter is linguistic.
2. **What happens when a judgment runs long?** (M-B2) — there is no abort, no owner, no threshold; only a percentile.
3. **How does an operator's spoken command reach the plant, and what is "commit"?** (M-B4) — the killer feature's guarantee is stated against an undefined event, and the only two candidate architectures have opposite implications for whether the LLM is in the control path.
4. **How many tokens may the plant spend?** (M-B1) — three numbers, no two of which agree.
5. **What stops the room from eating itself?** (M-B7/M-B8) — fusion ships auricle's measured-catastrophic configuration with one of three guards, and the acoustic echo path is unmentioned.
6. **Who renders events to text, on which thread?** (M-M11) — silence here invites a `sprintf` on the 100 µs tick.

Each is a paragraph, not a redesign. **§4 is one focused revision away from buildable** — the architecture is sound and the inheritance is mostly honest; what is missing is the mechanical detail that turns inherited *doctrine* into inherited *behaviour*. The estate-consistency result supports that read: 13 of 16 cited measurements are exactly right, which is unusually good and means the spec's authors were reading the receipts, not remembering them.

### Top 3 risks

1. **The membrane cannot afford the crisis it exists for.** M-M7 (≈115% GPU duty at measured disposition and crisis event rates) and M-M6 (a ~9 s molt blackout after ~10 minutes of crisis-rate traffic) compound: the two failure modes both peak at exactly the moment the Sentinel is the only reason the membrane is there. Neither is visible until M3, and M3's gate as written does not measure for them. **Mitigation:** pre-register M3's fire-rate report as a *load* test with a stated duty-cycle ceiling; add plant-lane boundary coalescing and molt-on-calm before M3, not after.

2. **The killer feature's guarantee is a race it loses at p95.** M-B5 (≈1.9 s pipeline into a ≈1.95 s window) plus M-B4 (undefined commit) means F-GHOST is simultaneously the project's most compelling demo and its least-defined falsifier. The fix is cheap and makes the demo *better* — a templated interjection on a ghost-confirmed disruption is both faster and more trustworthy than a generated one — but only if it is decided now. Decided later, it is decided by whatever the M4 session happens to build.

3. **The bet is gated on a falsifier nothing schedules.** M-B9: D-007 correctly quarantines unsolicited speech behind F-INSTINCT — the single best decision in the document — but no milestone creates F-INSTINCT's held-out set, no review valve is ported, and M3's gate does not require labels. The predictable outcome is that M5 arrives, the gate cannot be run, and the gate quietly softens. auricle already built the machinery (`--review`, the quarantined 39-item set); it needs one line in M3's gate to be inherited rather than re-derived.

**One structural observation.** The three deepest findings (M-B2, M-B3, M-M8) share a shape: a law is inherited by **name** in §11 while its **enforcement mechanism** is left behind. TinyVillage's Deadline Law is a fixed budget *plus an abort* plus a *structurally distinct effective tick*; its label-factory fence is a doctrine *plus `labelcheck`*; its rater law is blind grading *plus disjoint streams and a consistency floor*. §11 is an excellent instrument and should be extended by one column — **"enforced by"** — because a law with no mechanism is a law this project will discover it did not actually inherit, at the milestone where it mattered.
